# relicmink

Mink's bizarre adventure

Open `project.godot` in Godot 4.7. F5 starts a procedurally generated level in `main.tscn`. To try the controls, open `training.tscn` and run that scene with F6.

| Input | Action |
| --- | --- |
| A / D or Left / Right | Run and turn |
| Space | Tap for a short jump, hold for a higher jump, then press again in the air to double jump |
| Shift | Dash in the facing direction |
| H | Trigger hit and knockback on demand |

Use the raised platforms to test jumps and landings at different heights. Use a double jump or an air dash to cross the pit. Falling in returns you to the starting point. Touch the red spikes to test contact damage.

You can jump up to 100 ms after leaving a ledge. If a jump cannot start immediately, pressing Space up to 120 ms before landing queues a jump. When a double jump is available, Space uses it immediately.

The first jump gets higher the longer you hold Space during ascent. The double jump has a fixed height. Pressing Space during a dash switches into a jump if one is available. You get one air dash per airtime, and dashing keeps any unused double jump available.

See the [prefab JSON guide](docs/prefab-json.md) to add rooms, corridors, shafts, or tile aliases.

Open `scenes/boss_test.tscn` and press F6 to fight **구르는 잭 (Rolling Jack)**. R restarts the fight. The player has five health and Jack has 200. Aim the equipped gun with the mouse and hold the left mouse button to fire while moving, jumping, or dashing.

Jack has 200 health. He normally walks toward the player; beyond 260 pixels of horizontal separation he accelerates to 175 pixels/second, stays in pursuit until within 180 pixels, and can extend walking to at most 2.4 seconds before attacking. `Rolling_Start` frames 1?11 stay still; frame 12 launches him. Rolls favor the floor, with separate horizontal travel, downward acceleration, and a 34-pixel downward snap for descending steps. Airborne rolls fall instead of hovering. Flat floor contact does not consume rebounds or emit liquid; hard landings and wall impacts retain liquid rings, squash, dust, and shake. The higher-hop variant only hops toward an elevated player; skipping hops stay low and return quickly. Straight rolls retain three wall rebounds (four when enraged) and a ten-second limit. Other rolls retain six/eight impacts. Ring variations, attack warnings, and permanent enrage at 60% health remain in place. `tools/test_jack_ground_chase.gd` verifies pursuit, every ground-roll variant, ascending/descending ledges, airborne gravity, and one impact per hard landing.

The harder encounter uses roll speeds 460/580 and liquid speeds 210/273 before/after enrage. Walking lasts 1.15/0.65 seconds and the tired loop lasts 2.3/1.6 seconds; the vulnerable tired-entry animation remains intact. Drinking is faster and continues through nonlethal hits, including its final one-health recovery. Every second enraged cycle chains into a different roll before exhaustion; followups retain the full startup tell and are limited to two horizontal or three upward impacts. Alternate enraged rings redirect three existing drops toward the player's predicted position instead of adding projectiles. The encounter caps active liquid orbs at 96, including aimed fans. `tools/test_jack_difficulty.gd` verifies phase transitions, recovery windows, prediction, the cap, and a live second-phase combo.

Two additional attacks are interleaved as roll → aimed spray → roll → liquid mines. Spray reuses `Drink` without healing, gives a 0.65-second tell, and fires three retargeted fans of five drops (seven when enraged). Mines find the floor or raised platform near the player's position and show three pulsing liquid puddles before bursting upward after 0.9 seconds (0.7 when enraged). Both attacks end in exhaustion, use the shared liquid atlas, obey the existing projectile cap, and are removed on victory or defeat. The mine implementation is `scripts/jack_liquid_mine.gd`; `tools/test_jack_extra_patterns.gd` checks timing, retargeting, platform placement, damage, and cleanup.

Spray shows a thin orange fan before each shot; it turns red and locks the aim for the last 0.25 attack-time seconds (about 0.22 seconds in enrage). Shots follow that committed direction so the player can dodge the tell. Mine casts immediately mark the three actual terrain surfaces; those positions are fixed for the cast instead of relocating under the player at placement time. Each puddle keeps shrinking warning brackets until detonation. Warnings use a few lines at 12 visual updates per second, add no collision shapes or text, and are cleared on state changes or encounter end. `tools/test_jack_warnings.gd` checks warning timing, shot alignment, fixed mine placement, and cleanup.

Terrain impacts visibly compress Jack along the impact axis for 0.075 seconds, then stretch him as he relaunches. The final impact holds the same compression before exhaustion. Each collision emits pixel dust and triggers a short camera shake that settles back to zero; rolling near the floor also leaves a light dust trail. Dust uses drawing only and has no collision shapes. Camera offsets, dust, and enemy projectiles are cleared when the encounter ends.

Touching Jack causes damage during every living phase, including walking, exhaustion, and drinking. Stomping and dashing into him do not deal damage. Gunshots deal one damage while he walks and two during `Tired_Init`, `Tired_Loop`, and `Drink`; `Rolling_Start` and `Rolling_Loop` block shots. A completed drink restores one health even when shot; hits still deal double damage and do not reset the animation. Lethal shots still defeat Jack immediately. Bullets stop at terrain, and the gun fires once every 0.18 seconds. Victory and defeat are shown in the HUD.

The fight reuses the original sprite frames and tile terrain, with one shared `RectangleShape2D` resource for movement and contact detection. The gun and bullets use simple built-in drawing, and swept ray queries detect bullet collisions without adding collision polygons. Pattern speeds, health, recovery time, and arena limits are exported on `RollingJack`; behavior is in `scripts/rolling_jack.gd`, the equipped gun is in `scenes/ranged_weapon.tscn`, and the test HUD and restart are in `scripts/boss_test.gd`. `tools/test_ranged_combat.gd` checks shooting, terrain blocking, armor, drinking through damage, and victory.

Enemy liquid orbs use animated one-unit pixel cells (the shared atlas is rendered at half size), amber shading, highlights, a droplet tail, and a brief splash on impact. They travel outward without homing, stop at terrain or the player, expire after 3.4 seconds, respect existing hurt immunity, and are cleared on victory or defeat. Their drawing and swept collision are in `scripts/jack_liquid_orb.gd`; `tools/test_jack_liquid_patterns.gd` checks the two patterns, circular volleys, damage, blocking, expiration, and cleanup.

Liquid animation frames share `sprites/jack_liquid_atlas.png`; only frame changes trigger a redraw, and each orb reuses its physics query instead of allocating three queries every physics tick. `tools/bake_liquid_atlas.gd` regenerates the atlas from the original pixel drawing. `tools/benchmark_jack_liquid.gd` measures 96 orbs over 360 frames with `--headless --fixed-fps 60`. On the development machine, the original drawing averaged 44.50 ms/frame and physics 5.69 ms/frame; the cached version measured 0.17 ms/frame and 2.10 ms/frame respectively. These are instrumented script CPU timings in an empty scene, not gameplay FPS or GPU timings.

BossTest uses Jack at sprite scale 1 with a matching 135 × 107.5 collision shape. Its arena is 960 units wide, with the floor at y=432 and raised platforms at y=240. A fixed camera shows the whole arena. The player is scaled to 1.5 and shares the default movement tuning: run 210, jump 540, double jump 560, gravity 1150, and dash 560 for 0.28 seconds. A held jump clears Jack and a double jump reaches the platforms. Every player instance now uses these defaults; BossTest no longer duplicates the movement properties. Run `tools/test_rolling_jack.gd` and `tools/test_boss_movement.gd` with Godot's `--headless --script` and `--fixed-fps 60` options to check combat and actual player inputs.

Rolling Jack steps over grounded ledges up to 32 pixels high without losing speed, spending a rebound, or emitting an impact volley. The up/forward/down collision probes require a clear path and landing surface; tall walls, insufficient headroom, and airborne side impacts retain normal rebounds. Adjust `roll_step_height` on `RollingJack`. `tools/test_jack_steps.gd` covers both directions, 16/32-pixel steps, blocked steps, continued drinking, and lethal shots during Drink.

Dash afterimages reuse the current sprite frame in one renderer (`scripts/dash_trail.gd`), emit every 0.035 seconds, fade over 0.22 seconds, and retain at most seven snapshots. Facing, animation pose, scale, and world position are preserved. Respawn and encounter cleanup clear them. Ground rolls emit four large 10-18-pixel sand/gray dust puffs every 0.065 seconds. Puffs expand, lift, drift backward and fade, with a 0.56-second emitter lifetime and a fourteen-emitter limit shared with impact dust. Each puff uses a loose cluster of three differently sized square blocks, which spread apart while fading, with no texture or collider. Neither effect adds collision geometry.

Rendering uses a 960 by 540 viewport, nearest filtering, integer window scaling, and transform snapping. BossTest uses native 1x camera zoom and 2x HUD scale to retain its arena framing. The main scene uses integer zoom steps starting at 2x, keeps an independent smooth camera target, and snaps the rendered camera position to its pixel grid; Training retains its previous framing at 2x. Shake offsets snap to the same grid. `scripts/pixel_camera.gd` handles camera alignment. Smaller windows can crop the integer-sized viewport; resize to at least 960 by 540 for the full view. `tools/test_player_visuals.gd` checks common player stats, afterimage pose/cap/lifetime/cleanup, camera snapping and viewport settings, and bounded roll particles.

Liquid body, tail, impact splash, and mine puddles share the 0.5 liquid drawing scale. Orb edge sweeps use a 2.5-unit offset instead of 5 to match the smaller body; speed, damage, lifespan, and burst timing are unchanged.

Original synthesized retro sound effects live in `audio/sfx`; regenerate all 15 mono 22.05 kHz WAVs with `python tools/bake_sfx.py`. `GameAudio` preloads the bank and reuses ten one-shot voices on a limited SFX bus. Per-cue cooldowns and a two-voice cue cap prevent projectile splashes from stacking; hit and outcome cues can replace low-priority voices. Each boss owns one quiet, speed-pitched rolling loop, starting at launch and stopping during impact pauses, recovery, defeat, and scene teardown. Cues cover windup, launch, impact, liquid volleys/spray/mines/splashes, gunfire, player/boss hits, dash, drinking, and victory/defeat. Expiring drops remain silent. Automated headless runs load the bank but skip actual playback.
